# English, Tamil, and Hindi

These are starting criteria, not a rule that every writer must share one register. The source voice and the user's preferences take priority. For other languages, establish comparable language-specific choices; do not route them through English by default.

## English

Use precise, contemporary literary English with room for the source's strangeness. Avoid a stock mystical or devotional voice for Indian poetry. Plain verbs can carry satire and force more accurately than grand Latinate nouns. Keep rhetorical questions active; do not explain the metaphor after presenting it. Retain culturally meaningful names or roles where an English substitution would erase their relationship. Watch articles and explicit pronouns: English may require them grammatically, but they should not falsely resolve the original's ambiguity. Keep intentionally repeated words instead of polishing them into synonyms.

## Tamil

Aim for readable literary Tamil appropriate to the source's period and voice, not word-for-word Odia syntax, machine-like nominal chains, or automatically archaic diction. Modern literary language does not require a casual spoken register. Choose natural verb endings, agreement, pronouns, and respectful forms consistently. Do not impose elevated honorifics where the source is ironic or socially distant. Avoid both automatic Sanskrit substitution and rigid lexical purism; meaning, intelligibility, and voice govern the choice. Preserve Odia cultural identity rather than relocating the scene to Tamil culture. Rework line breaks around complete syntactic and emotional units, retaining the source's turns. A fluent Tamil reader should examine idiom, register, and cadence before publication.

## Hindi

Use natural literary Hindi with a consistent register. Do not default to heavily Sanskritized diction or a generic bhajan voice because the source contains mythology. Avoid mechanically transferring Odia word order, participles, or case relations. Check gender, number, agreement, pronouns, and honorific choices against agency and power relations. Do not let respectful grammar neutralize satire. Balance familiar vocabulary with exact cultural terms; retain Odia identities when a Hindi equivalent changes the reference. Re-create rhythm through syntax and sound without adding rhyme or heroic cadence. A fluent Hindi reader should check whether a phrase sounds composed in Hindi rather than converted into it.

## Across all three

One source image can have different natural syntax in each language. Consistency means preserving its role and meaning, not selecting the same cognate everywhere. Keep a private glossary with context and alternatives; allow a recurring word to change meaning from poem to poem. When uncertain, show the phrase and explain the specific choice instead of assigning a numerical fluency score.

Audience recognition matters in each language. Review the situation and the implied feeling, not only grammatical fluency. An English, Tamil or Hindi idiom should preserve the relationship, humor or sting; a familiar phrase with a stronger accusation or devotional association is not an equivalent. Preserve culturally meaningful names and objects while making their role intelligible through natural phrasing. Consult cultural-effect.md when a material substitution is being considered.
