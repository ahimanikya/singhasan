# Twelve Indian languages: translation review

Use the relevant language section when translating into or out of this user-selected set: **Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Telugu, Tamil, Gujarati, Urdu, Kannada, Odia, Malayalam, Assamese and Punjabi**. This extends the eleven-language set in the user’s supplied image with Punjabi, subsequently requested by the user. English remains a separate supported language. Population percentages are not translation-quality evidence and are not reproduced here.

These are review prompts, not full grammars or a claim of independently verified fluency. Retain the shared [language principles](languages.md) and [personification lesson](poetic-review-lessons.md#personification-and-author-feedback). Select the target variety and register from the actual brief and source. Read each target directly against the source; do not route all twelve through Hindi or English.

## Hindi

Check gender-bearing adjectives, participles and verb forms against the noun or participant controlling agreement. Separate grammatical gender from a village or landscape’s poetic identity. Preserve pronoun ambiguity where the source leaves it open, and do not infer the speaker’s gender from the author. Use natural literary Hindi without automatically adding Sanskritized solemnity. Apply the Shakuntala example contextually, never as a blanket गाँव → टोली substitution.

## Bengali

Bengali personal pronouns do not ordinarily distinguish he from she, and verbs do not use Hindi-style gender agreement. When translating out of Bengali, avoid supplying a participant’s gender without evidence; when translating into it, retain meaningful feminine or masculine personification through the image and context. Check intimacy and respect in pronoun/verb choices rather than treating all equivalents of “you” as interchangeable. Preserve the source’s register rather than automatically making it archaic. [Pronoun translation study](https://aclanthology.org/W12-5214.pdf), [Bengali grammar features](https://universaldependencies.org/bn/index.html).

## Marathi

Marathi has masculine, feminine and neuter grammatical genders. Check the actual noun’s gender and the agreement required by the construction; do not carry Hindi assignments across by resemblance. Neuter grammatical marking is not evidence that a referent lacks human identity. Preserve personification, respectful address and the source’s balance of colloquial and literary language without adding devotional intensity. [Gender inventory](https://wals.info/valuesets/30A-mhi).

## Telugu

Review third-person pronouns, number, honorific usage and verb forms together. Feminine and neuter singular forms can share verbal endings, so an ending alone does not establish an object-like referent or loss of personification. Do not import Tamil’s agreement pattern. Match the intended spoken or literary register and preserve the poem’s cadence without forcing rhyme or ornamental compounds. Verify contemporary usage for the target variety; older grammatical descriptions are background, not a modern style prescription. [Historical Telugu grammar](https://te.wikisource.org/wiki/A_grammar_of_the_Telugu_language/BOOK_FIRST).

## Tamil

Check third-person pronouns and finite-verb agreement, including ordinary non-human reference versus deliberate personification. Do not assign every object a Hindi-style masculine or feminine identity. Preserve respectful forms only where the source relationship supports them; neutral or respectful wording must not accidentally erase irony or intimacy. Retain Odia cultural anchors when translating Odia rather than relocating the scene. [Verb agreement](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/plc/tamilweb/book/chapter3/node32.html).

## Gujarati

Gujarati has masculine, feminine and neuter noun genders. Check adjective and verbal agreement in the actual construction, including which argument controls it; a similar Hindi word is not sufficient evidence. Do not replace a culturally meaningful object simply to select a preferred gender. Preserve natural Gujarati phrasing and the source’s register rather than transliterating Hindi syntax into Gujarati script. [Gujarati morphology](https://aclanthology.org/W10-3607.pdf).

## Urdu

Review noun gender and dependent agreement without assuming every Hindi cognate behaves identically. Preserve personification while retaining the original relationship, image and social tone. Choose vocabulary for the poem’s voice; do not automatically Persianize or romanticize plain language. Use the requested script, ordinarily Urdu script for an Urdu target; transliteration is a separate choice. [Urdu noun grammar](https://urdu.la.utexas.edu/resources/plurality-in-urdu/).

## Kannada

Gender assignment is substantially semantic, with human distinctions and culturally established exceptions; do not classify every noun by endings borrowed from Sanskrit or another language. Check pronouns and agreement when an otherwise non-human referent is personified. Preserve address, respect and the intended literary or colloquial voice without inventing mythological attributes to explain a grammatical choice. [Kannada gender assignment](https://wals.info/chapter/32).

## Odia

Third-person pronouns do not distinguish male from female. Gender can still be meaningful in lexical choices, relationships and imagery. Translating out of Odia must not turn an unspecified beloved or speaker into an assumed man or woman; translating into it must not erase a source’s meaningful identity. Preserve dialect, idiom, cultural objects and stanza turns; do not normalize a difficult word merely because a Hindi cognate seems familiar. [Odia pronoun study](https://kumadai.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2000478/files/KG0022_019-048.pdf).

## Malayalam

Gender distinctions in personal reference do not imply Tamil-style gender agreement on finite verbs. Check pronouns and lexical choices independently rather than adding gendered verb endings. Preserve distinctions of respect and intimacy, natural compound phrasing and the source’s register; do not use literary density as a substitute for poetic force. Read personification through the complete passage rather than treating an unchanged verb as a translation defect. [Malayalam morphology research](https://aclanthology.org/L18-1413.pdf), [Malayalam grammar features](https://universaldependencies.org/ml/index.html).

## Assamese

Do not group Assamese with Bengali as uniformly gender-neutral in its pronouns: forms such as সি and তাই distinguish male/female reference. Review the actual pronoun, respect level and context before supplying or removing a gender distinction. Preserve Assamese idiom, spelling and voice rather than treating shared script or related vocabulary as permission to copy Bengali phrasing. A neutral or respectful recasting must preserve the source relationship. [Assamese pronoun examples, AIIMS Guwahati](https://aiimsguwahati.ac.in/uploads/publications/AMFE.pdf).

## Punjabi

Check the actual noun’s masculine/feminine gender and the agreement required by its construction, including adjectives, participles and verbs where applicable. Do not assume a Hindi or Urdu cognate has identical gender or usage. Preserve poetic personification without assigning the speaker or beloved a gender that the source leaves open. Keep intimacy, respect and number consistent in forms of address; choose a natural recasting when agreement would force an unsupported interpretation.

Use the audience’s requested script and variety: Gurmukhi and Shahmukhi are writing systems for Punjabi, not separate target languages. Infer script from supplied materials and stated audience where clear; ask only when the missing choice materially affects the deliverable. Preserve Punjabi idiom and cadence rather than converting Hindi wording into another script. Do not automatically turn plain verse into folk-song, devotional or highly Persianized diction. [Gender assignment](https://wals.info/valuesets/32A-pan), [Punjabi and its scripts](https://www.southasia.upenn.edu/languages/explore-languages/punjabi-language).

## Apply without mechanical replacement

For the chosen language, trace the figure through its title, repeated nouns, pronouns, possessives and relevant agreement. Compare what the source specifies, what the target requires, and what remains uncertain. Retain intentional shifts and natural ambiguity. If a target forces a consequential unsupported choice, record that exact question and continue independent work.

Keep source text, author feedback, provisional wording and approved corrections distinct. Record language/variety, source fingerprint, actual review scope and unresolved choices. A fluent-reader check, author approval and AI self-review are different events; coverage of these twelve languages in a skill does not claim that any poem has passed those reviews.
